# Staff passkeys — controlled enrollment release

## Scope and rollout status

The owner authorized native passkeys inside the existing application, two staff identities, private storage, deployment of enrollment, and retirement of legacy access **after both owners verify real-phone sign-in and recovery**. This release builds on main `0219fda`, preserving payments, email/SMS, editorial copy/portrait, layout, the original Inbox and public customer links. No customer accounts, paid authentication vendor, new service, customer messages, payments or historical migration are introduced.

The rollout deliberately has two states. `STAFF_PASSKEY_MODE=transition` enables passkeys only at `https://admin.fluffytailshihtzu.com`, and keeps legacy access temporarily on the current `www` hostname. `cutover` rejects legacy Basic credentials, old password-derived cookies and owner-access grants everywhere, redirects old staff pages to the admin unlock screen, and denies old-host staff APIs. Do not set `cutover` merely because automated checks pass. Both actual phones and recovery checks are required first.

## Enrollment for Jeanne and Jason / DjinX

1. On the owner's phone, open `https://www.fluffytailshihtzu.com/inbox/security` with the existing staff sign-in. Choose **Create Jeanne’s invitation** or **Create Jason’s invitation** for the owner holding that phone. Only these two identities exist; there is no registration endpoint that accepts a new staff identity.
2. Choose **Continue enrollment on this device**. The invitation moves to the admin hostname in a POST body and an HttpOnly cookie, never a URL. Choose **Create my passkey** and complete the phone's Face ID/fingerprint/PIN or passcode prompt. The phone controls the available verification method; the application cannot force a particular biometric or receive biometric data.
3. Save the recovery codes displayed privately in the application. Download them into an owner-controlled secure location or save them in the owner's password manager, separate from the phone. Enter one saved code to verify recovery possession. This consumes that code and signs out for a real sign-in check. Codes are never supplied in chat, source or logs.
4. Choose **Unlock with a passkey**, sign in, open both the staff workspace and original Inbox, refresh and navigate. Confirm this on the actual iPhone/Android device for each owner. From **Staff access & recovery**, verify that both owner rows show passkey saved, sign-in verified and recovery verified.
5. If using a laptop to issue an invitation for another phone, the private application displays the owner-bound invitation for direct transfer. On the phone open the admin unlock screen, choose **Set up with an owner invitation**, select that owner and paste it. Invitations expire after 30 minutes, are single use, and reissuing one invalidates the earlier invitation. An enrolled identity cannot be re-enrolled through legacy access.

Adding a passkey, revoking one, rotating recovery codes or ending another session requires passkey verification within the last five minutes. Normal correspondence does not require repeated verification. The access screen offers **Verify passkey again** when needed. A last active passkey cannot be removed until a replacement is added. Recovery permits only access management and replacement-key enrollment; it does not expose business records. A used recovery code retires all prior passkey/session epochs for that owner, including a lost phone, and cannot be reused. Recovery codes may be replaced after a new passkey is enrolled.

If a code-display response is interrupted or codes were not saved, the owner can sign in with their successfully registered passkey and create replacement recovery codes. Clear saved codes from the screen when finished. Never ask either owner to paste a code, invitation or credential into chat.

## Verification and storage

`@simplewebauthn/server` 14.0.3 and `@simplewebauthn/browser` 14.0.0 perform WebAuthn option generation, browser ceremonies and cryptographic verification. Production RP ID is exactly `admin.fluffytailshihtzu.com`, expected origin is exactly its HTTPS origin, resident credentials and user verification are required, attestation is none, and embedded/cross-origin ceremonies are rejected. Supported credential algorithms are ES256 and RS256. The application never implements signature cryptography or stores private authenticator keys.

Credential public keys/counters, the two owner handles, hashed invitations, hashed recovery codes, browser-bound ceremonies, session records and distributed rate counters use authenticated-only `private.staff*` Sanity IDs. All are namespaced separately for production and synthetic Preview. Challenge claims and invitation/owner/credential/session updates use revision checks and transactions. Challenges last five minutes and are consumed even when verification subsequently fails. Counter checks and revision claims prevent replay and concurrent duplicate sessions. Discoverable sign-in does not publish a list of registered owners' credential IDs.

Staff sessions use an independent random `STAFF_SESSION_SECRET`, signed opaque random cookies and private revocable records, not the Basic password. Production cookies are `__Host-` prefixed, Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict, path `/`, with no Domain attribute. Sessions expire after 30 days absolutely or seven days without activity. Revocation, disabled owners, credential revocation and epoch changes are checked against storage before access. Hourly activity updates use revision checks; storage failures fail closed. A per-address/action limit of 20 and global/action limit of 200 in ten minutes persists across serverless workers. Only hashes of address/action keys are stored, never raw IPs. Expired security records are inert; no unrelated data cleanup or historical migration is performed.

Proxy routing checks are repeated in the staff server layout and independently by every original Inbox/workspace API, including uploads/downloads and payment/message handlers. Unauthorized requests stop before customer queries or provider calls. State-changing access requests enforce exact Origin and JSON type/size limits. The narrowly scoped same-site invitation handoff accepts only POST from the current `www` host and grants only an enrollment capability, still subject to a valid invitation and verified WebAuthn ceremony.

## Final cutover runbook — wait for both owners

1. Read each owner's private readiness row: actual passkey enrollment, subsequent sign-in, saved-code verification, and the owner's real phone/Inbox/navigation confirmation. A simulated authenticator is not sufficient. Check recovery works through the application without exposing codes to a developer. Add another passkey per owner when practical.
2. Inventory **all** Vercel deployments and aliases with pagination. Keep `all_except_custom_domains` Vercel Authentication in place. Remove old share links, scoped bypasses and alias protection overrides for deployments that could reach Production. Do not assume changing environment variables removes secrets embedded in immutable deployments. Record what happens to the old synthetic payment-test URL separately; preserve its records even if its old authentication entry is retired.
3. Apply and publish the reviewed project-wide staff-path boundary in `scripts/staff-access-boundary.json`. It denies `/inbox` paths on every hostname other than admin/current www, across historical and future deployments. This prevents historical URLs and alias/share bypasses from reusing old application authentication. Public inquiries, `/pay`, Stripe/Twilio webhooks and customer pages are outside the rule. Check encoded and RSC staff paths against the actual platform normalization as well as direct paths. If the platform cannot enforce this boundary on historical deployments, protect/retire those deployments individually before declaring cutover complete; do not leave a known reachable old entry.
4. Change Production mode to `cutover`, remove the Production legacy Basic settings (preserve unrelated environment scopes), and deploy the verified code from current main. Keep the new session Secret unchanged. Remove/invalidate unused legacy owner-access records only within their authorization scope; their route is already disabled. Do not delete business records or financial history.
5. Confirm old passwords, old cookies, old owner-access grants, current default/branch aliases, representative historical production deployments and every bypass-bearing URL cannot access staff HTML, RSC data or direct APIs. The `www` pages redirect to admin and its staff APIs deny access. New admin passkey sessions continue to work, revoke immediately, and remain private after sign-out. Public inquiries stay enabled and public payment pages/webhooks retain their existing validators. No customer messages, new Checkout sessions, payments or webhook resends are needed for verification.
6. Remove the transition invitation UI path after readiness, retain passkey/recovery management, and document the exact deployment, containment configuration and actual-phone evidence. Never report the old access retired while still in transition.
